Deliveries pay about 300 to 600 each, every 25 to 35 s. So the best way to score per minute is to hug a wall and ignore the parcels. The round 13 fix capped the clock that drafting buys. It did not touch the points.

**Why it matters most.** Thirteen rounds tuned lines and rates around this split. Each fix moves the hole. While drafting pays separately from delivering, the medals measure wall hugging as much as courier play. This is the one gap that stops the core loop from being one loop.

**A direction (not tested).** Make drafting pay through the parcel. For example, slip payouts on a leg go into a "tip" on the carried parcel, shown on the parcel or the objective pill. The tip is paid on delivery, multiplied by the combo. If time runs out, the tip is lost. Payouts can still refill boost at once, so drafting still feels good right away. With no parcel, drafting gives boost only.

## 1. Design

The round 12 slow clock had no limit and no link to the objective. I added two limits. Payouts, crash rules, roof-line flight and drop time are unchanged.

1. **Only on the way.** The slow clock runs only while the drone flies toward its current objective (pickup or drop). The game compares the drone's horizontal velocity with the direction to the target:
   - cos 0.2 or more (within about 80 degrees): full effect.
   - cos -0.1 or less: no effect.
   - In between: a linear ramp.
   - Config: `towardLow`, `towardFull`.
2. **A budget per leg.** Each leg adds at most `clockBudget` = **8 s** of clock.
   - A pickup or a delivery refills the budget to 8 s. It does not stack.
   - A run with no deliveries therefore gets at most 16 s in total: the start budget plus one pickup. A farm that never picks up gets 8 s.
   - Honest pilots never hit the limit. In round 12 the routed street bot used about 3 to 4 s per leg (34 to 38 s over 11 legs on seed 22).
3. The rate (`clockRate` 1), the pace thresholds and the decay per delivery are unchanged. I tried a rate of 1.5 on seed 58 to lift the weak low seed. That run scored 1,542: 11 s more clock, still 2 drops, and one drop was not fast. So I reverted it (`t-low58-r15.log`).

**What the player sees.** The badge on the timer now shows the budget left on the leg:
- "CLOCK FROZEN · 4S" or "CLOCK X0.1 · 5S".
- A good draft that earns no clock shows a muted badge, and the teal ring and glow switch off:
  - "SLOW CLOCK SPENT": the budget is used up.
  - "SLOW CLOCK: WRONG WAY": the drone flies away from the objective.
- Shots: `d-/m-clock-hug-cruise.jpg` and `d-/m-clock-hug-spent.jpg`. The title overlay is still fading in these shots. That comes from the test API, as in round 12.

Status: **incomplete. The acceptance test was not finished.** I ran out of time. The code is in, it type-checks and builds. The tuning is not proven. Read section 4 before you trust any number.

## 1. Design

Today every slipstream payout adds 0.5 s. Deliveries add 7 to 20 s. The roof line delivers more often, so its runs last longer and it wins the medals.

New rule: a payout adds clock time by **chain level** and by **pace**.

- Every payout still gives the base **0.5 s**.
- A chained payout adds up to `chainSeconds[chain - 1]` = **0, 3, 3, 3.5, 3.5** s (chain x1 to x5).
- That extra time is scaled by the meter's **pace**: draft metres per second while drafting. This is the same measure as the HUD chevrons. At or below **5** it adds nothing (a slow, centred crawl). From **11** it adds all of it (fast and close to a facade). The result is rounded to half seconds.
- **Budget per delivery:** the slipstream can add at most **10 s** between two drops. The budget shrinks with the run in the same way as the drop time bonus (x0.965 per delivery, floor 0.55). This cap keeps drafting from making a run endless. When the budget is used up, the pop says "time full, deliver".

# Drone Dash, round 11 builder: music audio quality

Scope: the procedural music in `src/systems/AudioSystem.ts` and `src/systems/music.ts`, plus the master level. No gameplay or scoring code changed. The bloom clamps in `src/entities/ObjectiveMarker.ts` and all debug hooks are untouched (one test hook added).

## 1. Cause of the clicks

**Cause: a GainNode starts at gain 1.** Every note made a new GainNode and set its envelope with `setValueAtTime(0.0001, t)`. Before that first event, the node passes signal at its default gain of 1. The step grid adds 0.12 s again and again. So a note often starts a tiny bit (around 1e-12 s) after a sample frame. Chromium then starts the source on that frame but applies the envelope's first event one frame later. So one sample of the source got through at full level. On an oscillator that sample is near zero. On a noise hat or clap it is a full-scale random value. The 7.2 kHz hat filter (or the 1.5 kHz clap filter) rings it into a short spike.

Evidence:
- **Shape and timing in the round 10 capture** (the raw capture in the session scratchpad): all 118 music-bus clicks sit at the same phase of the 0.12 s step grid (48.06 ms). Each one is a 6 to 7 sample ringing at about 7 kHz, which is the impulse response of the hat bandpass.
- **Reproduced offline** (`builder-scripts/click-repro.mjs`, log `click-repro.log`): 40 hats on a grid, in an OfflineAudioContext.

  | Start offset from the frame | Round 10 envelope (spikes / 40) | Fix, gain 0 at creation |
  |---|---|---|
  | 0 (0.1 + k x 0.02, float error only) | 4 | 0 |
  | +-1e-12 s | 20 | 0 |
  | +-1e-10 s | 19 | 0 |
  | +-1e-8 s | 20 | 0 |
  | +-3e-6 s | 0 | 0 |

  Spike size: up to 0.46. Fixed: under 0.003. The round 10 critic's per-voice render started every voice exactly on a sample frame. That is why it found nothing.
- **Why the rate grew with the music:** layer 1 has no noise voices, so 0 clicks. Chain 3 adds off-beat hats and claps, so about 0.7 a second. The last 10 s put a hat on every 16th note plus claps, so about 4 a second.

**Fix at the source:** `envGain()` makes each note's gain node with `gain.value = 0`. It is used by every music voice (`mNote`, `kick`, `hit`) and by the SFX `tone` and `noise`.

**A second, smaller source showed up at the louder level:** master-only clicks during boost. I tapped each input of the master bus on its own channel (`builder-scripts/bisect-master.mjs`): SFX, reverb, rotor, wind, draft and music. Only the SFX bus had them, as periodic edges at 220 to 440 a second. That is the boost whine, a plain `sawtooth` with a one-sample edge every period. It now uses a soft saw (12 harmonics with a Lanczos taper). After the fix all six channels and their sum show 0. I first suspected the rotor saw. An offline rotor render (`rotor-edges.mjs`) ruled it out, and the rotor is unchanged.

Other edges were removed on the way:
- The scene fade used to set a target of 0, then cut the tail with `setValueAtTime(0)`. It is now a straight 40 ms ramp to exactly 0.

# Drone Dash, round 10 builder: adaptive music

Concern: the game had no music. The title was silent, and nothing in the audio followed the chain, the clock or a medal chase.

## Musical design

- **Key and tempo.** C major at 125 BPM, on a 16th-note grid (one step = 0.12 s). The medal fanfare uses the same grid: its notes are 0.12 s apart. The start sting notes are 0.06 s apart. The pickup and delivery chimes are C major. The slipstream ticks climb D-F-Ab-B, and the no-medal game-over sting falls C-Ab-F-C. So the borrowed chords Ab, Bb and Fm come from sounds that are already in the game.
- **Title loop.** Fmaj7, Em7, Dm7, Gsus, two bars per chord (about 15 s). It has a soft triangle pad and a bass note on each bar. A plucked eighth-note arpeggio has a dotted-eighth echo. Each chord gets one high bell. There are no drums. It fades in over 1.2 s after the first press.
- **Run track.** C, Am, F, G, one chord per bar. The layers build with the slipstream chain:

| Chain | Section (`audio.music.section`) | `layer` | Layers playing | Target intensity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | base | 1 | pad + syncopated bass | 0.30 |
| 1 | drums | 2 | + kick on 1 and 3, offbeat hats, octave bass pickup | 0.50 |
| 2 | arp | 3 | + four-on-the-floor kick, eighth-note arpeggio | 0.68 |
| 3+ | lead | 4 | + claps on 2 and 4, 16th arpeggio, ghost hats, lead melody | 0.85 |

  A close medal chase (the last 15% of the way to the next medal) adds ghost hats and +0.05 intensity. The pad lowpass opens from 450 Hz to 2.1 kHz as intensity rises (smoothed, about 0.6 s).
- **Last 10 s ("final", layer 5).** The harmony changes to Ab, Bb, Ab, G7. The bass plays driving eighth-note octaves. The section adds four-on-the-floor, claps, 16th hats, a 16th arpeggio and a ticking high C on the eighths. Target intensity is 0.92 to 0.98. The change lands on the next beat, so the groove stays in time. If a time bonus lifts the clock above 12 s, the run section comes back. The gap between 10 s and 12 s stops the music from flipping back and forth.
- **Game over.** The sting depends on the medal. With no medal it is the old falling C-Ab-F-C. With a medal it is a rising G-C-E with a held C major chord and one bell for each tier. A slow loop then swells in over 1.8 s under the sting:
  - No medal ("calm"): Fm, C, Am, Gsus with a falling sigh note.
  - With a medal: C, Fmaj7, Am, G with bells that climb the chord. Silver adds a falling line. Gold and Ace add a quiet octave sparkle.
- **Ducking.** The music bus has its own duck gain. Each cue sets a depth and a hold time, and overlapping ducks keep the deeper level and the later end.

| Cue | Duck level | Hold |
|---|---|---|
| Medal fanfare (timed to the banner delay) | 0.28 (−11 dB) | 1.25 s |
| Crash | 0.35 | 0.55 s |
| Delivery | 0.45 | 0.7 s |
| Game-over sting | 0.5 | 1.0 s |
| Pickup | 0.6 | 0.35 s |
| Slip payout | 0.7 | 0.25 s |

- **Mix and safety.** The music runs at 0.5 of the 0.8 master. It goes through a 55 Hz highpass and a 6.5 kHz lowpass, so there are no harsh highs. Small speakers cannot play notes below 55 Hz, and those notes waste headroom. Hats are band-passed at 7.2 kHz at very low level. Bass sits in octave 2-3 (73 to 130 Hz) with triangle harmonics, so phones still reproduce it. The master is now glue compressor (−14 dB, 4:1), then a brick-wall limiter (−2 dB, 20:1, 2 ms attack). The music never uses the `square` oscillator. The fanfare brass stays the only square wave below 1 kHz, which the critic's fanfare count depends on.
- **Scheduler.** A lookahead scheduler on `AudioContext.currentTime` schedules 0.2 s ahead. It wakes on a 25 ms interval and also on every game frame. There is no setTimeout per note. A note scheduled with a start time in the past counts as `late`. If the timer was starved for more than 0.25 s, the grid skips forward. That skip counts as `resyncs`, and no burst of notes plays. When music is off, nothing is scheduled. On the title and game-over screens a hidden tab suspends the context. When the tab is visible again, the context resumes. Cost is about 40 short oscillator or noise nodes a second at full intensity. There is one shared filter per drum voice and one echo delay. No second convolver was added.
- **Gesture and title.** Any pointerdown, touchend, click or keydown anywhere (the title card too) starts the AudioContext. The title loop then plays. Pressing Play still starts the run in one press. Until then a pulsing amber chip at the top right reads "Click or press a key for sound" (desktop) or "Tap for sound" (touch).
- **Music toggle.** The chip turns into "Music on" or "Music off" after the first press. It shows on the title, pause (below the pause button) and game-over screens. The M key toggles music anywhere. The setting is stored in localStorage `drone-dash-music-v1`, wrapped in try/catch. It only affects music: SFX and the rotor are unchanged. There was no existing sound toggle.

## Design

### Medals (score goals)

There are four medals for one run's score: **Bronze 1,000, Silver 2,250, Gold 4,500, Ace 7,500** (`GOALS` in config.ts).

Why these thresholds (deterministic sim, plus real-time checks, seeds 22, 58, 130):
- Lazy pilot runs: 1,723 / 791 / 255 (real time: 1,728 / 816 / 1,141). Silver sits above the best lazy run with a margin of 30%. A slow, centred flier gets Bronze at most.
- Standard low runs: 3,688 / 1,792 / 2,782 (real time: 3,553 / 1,792 / 2,537). Silver in 2 of 3.
- High runs: 6,859 / 4,123 / 4,760 (real time: 5,524 / 4,078 / 5,421). Gold in 2 of 3, Silver in 3 of 3.
- Ace is 9% above the best bot run seen (6,859) and 36% above the best real-time bot run. Ace needs a long, clean run with a deep combo.
- The steps roughly double (1,000, 2,250, 4,500, 7,500), so each medal feels like a new level.

Where the medals show:
- **Title:** a row of four medal chips with names and scores, under Play. Chips for medals already won get a gold outline. The best line reads "Best 3,733 Silver".
- **In-run HUD:** one compact row under the score, in the top-left corner: next medal disc, its name and score ("SILVER 2,250"), and a thin 56 px progress bar from the last medal. No change to the area below the drone, which was already crowded on mobile.
- **Medal moment:** a large medal banner shows ("SILVER!"). The medal flips in, rays turn behind it, and a pill says "Next: Gold 4,500". A three-note fanfare plays. It rises one tone per medal and adds one bell per medal. A sparkle burst in the medal colour comes off the drone, the camera shakes a little, and the HUD goal row flashes. A drop usually carries the score across a line. So if a delivery pop is still showing, the banner waits until that pop has shown for about 1 s, then replaces it. The sound waits with it. On short (phone) screens the medal sits beside the text, so the banner stays clear of the objective pill.
- **Game over:** medal reached (or "No medal yet"), the score, "New best!", "767 more for Gold", then a breakdown:
  - Deliveries ×n (100 + 1.2 per metre of each leg)
  - Fast drops ×n (+75 each, before the combo)
  - Combo bonus (up to x2.5): the extra points from the combo multiplier
  - Slipstream ×payouts
  - Score (total)
  
  The game counts each line as a whole number at the moment it is earned. So the lines sum exactly to the score. Below that is the clock story: "Flew 1:48. 50 s start, +48 s drops, +10 s slipstream, −3 s 1 crash". Crashes cost seconds, not points, so they show in the time line. The time line sums exactly to the seconds flown. It also explains why street runs are shorter. Then come the best score and best medal, and "Fly again".
- **Storage:** the best score is in `drone-dash-best-v1` (unchanged). The best medal is in `drone-dash-best-tier-v1`. Each read and write is in try/catch.
- **Diagnostics:** `goals = { tier, tierName, next, passes, passList: [{tier, name, score, t}], best, bestTier, thresholds }` and `breakdown = { drops, fast, combo, slip, fastCount, dropSeconds, slipSeconds, crashSeconds, total }`. When one drop crosses two lines, both are counted.

I did not move the desktop slipstream meter. It was optional, and I kept the risk low.

### Leftover A: mobile first payout

On the first meter of a run only, the speed factor is at least 0.6 (`introSpeedFloor`) once the drone is above 4 m/s. So speed counts once on the intro meter, not twice. A touch pilot drafting at 6 m/s now fills the 4-metre intro meter in about 1.5 s. Every later meter is unchanged, so the lazy pilot gains one early 45-point payout and nothing more.

### Leftover B: strict slipstream share

Every point that exists only because of drafting is now in the "Slipstream" line. So the strict share and the direct share are the same number:
- **Street multiplier removed.** A slipstream chain held at the drop no longer multiplies the delivery.
- **Par pause removed.** Drafting no longer stops the fast-drop clock.
- **Par loosened** so that the street route can make it without drafting: par = street-route length / 9 m/s + 7 s (was / 12.5 + 5 with the draft pause). The standard low bot makes 7 of 7 fast drops, the same as before with the pause. The high line already made every par.
- **Payouts smaller:** 45 + 25 per chain step, so x5 pays 145. Before, it was 50 + 30, and x5 paid 170.

Why the 0.9 ratio cannot also be met: if the share is at most 50%, low score can be at most 2 × low non-draft points. Low non-draft points are about 900 a minute in the sim (about 900 real time). High is about 2,200 a minute. So the best possible ratio at a 50% share is about 0.80. In real time I got 0.76 at a 46% share. The low bot delivers 1.48 a minute against 2.62 for the high bot, and the high bot builds deeper combos (x3.5). Per-delivery changes scale both lines alike. I tested a combo cap of 3 or 4, a stronger headwind and other combo curves in the sim. None reaches 0.9 at a 50% share without cutting the high line below about 2.3 deliveries a minute or gutting the combo. The only other way is a new non-draft reward for the street route (for example a bonus for a leg flown under the roofline). I did not add it. It can look like the street multiplier moved into a new category. This is a choice for the next round: accept the ratio, weaken the roof line, or add a street-route reward.

# Drone Dash, round 8 builder

Concern: the slipstream paid for time spent near walls, not for flying well. A lazy pilot (centred, 10 m/s, no boost) scored the most per minute.

## Design

1. **The meter fills by distance, and speed counts twice.** Fill per second = draft quality x speed (m/s), so it is "draft metres". Draft quality = wall factor x speed factor. The speed factor is 0 below 4 m/s and full from 13 m/s. So 16 m/s fills about 4 to 7 times faster than 9 m/s on the same street, and a crawl barely fills.
2. **The street centre is no longer full strength.** The wall factor is full only within 4 m of a facade (was 9.5 m). The street centre (9 m from both facades) is about 0.7. Hugging a facade (inside 5 m, full at 2.5 m) adds up to 1.6x on top. Reach is unchanged at 16 m.
3. **A chain needs quick payouts.** The chain also resets when the next payout takes more than 9 s. A crawl cannot build x3 to x5. The chain is still lost on a charged crash, and after 6 s out of the draft.
4. **The par pause no longer rewards slowness.** The fast-drop clock only pauses while draft quality is at least 0.55, which means fast and near a facade. The lazy pilot (10 m/s, centred, about 0.47) no longer pauses it.
5. **Deliveries carry the street reward ("street multiplier").** A delivery pays +15% per slipstream chain step held at the drop (x5 = x1.75). The drop pop reads, for example, "Fast drop · street x1.6". These points count as delivery score. Diagnostics track them on their own as `slipstream.hotPoints`. I report them on their own lines below so the critic can judge the split.
6. **Payouts:** 50 points, plus 30 per chain step (x5 = 170). Meter sizes are 14 draft metres at chain 0, plus 4 per chain step. The first meter of a run is 4 draft metres (`introMeter`), so a new player gets a payout about 2 s after the first real draft.
7. **The HUD teaches the rule while you fly.**
   - Three chevrons next to "Slipstream" light up with fill speed. A crawl lights 1, the street centre at cruise 2, a wall hug or boosting 3.
   - A one-word cue above the meter names the limit: "Faster!" (near walls but too slow; this also shows the meter while you are not drafting), "Closer to wall" (fast but centred), or "Wall hug!".
   - Cues are hidden within 25 m of the pad and while the first-run hint shows.
   - The first-run hint now reads "Faster & closer to walls fills it faster". The title tagline reads "Fly fast and low, close to the walls, to ride the slipstream."
   - On mobile the cue sits on its own line above the meter, so it does not collide with the label (see m-* shots).

Not changed: flight, headwind, the high line, crash rules, arrival help, street-distance par formula, the bloom clamps in ObjectiveMarker.ts, and debug hooks. The diagnostics only add fields.

# Drone Dash, round 5 builder report: fair crash classification

## Cause

The round 4 crash check fired on the game's own skim helpers. Four separate faults caused this:

1. **The ledge hop counted as a crash.** A hop cancels the drone's speed into the ledge. The crash check then counted that speed as "lost".
2. **The speed loss was a raw before/after delta.** It included drag, the S brake and the drop guide's pull near pads. So near a pad, a light touch plus braking was enough to pass 7 m/s.
3. **Edge clips counted as wall hits.** Sometimes the drone's underside clipped a roof edge or the rim of a box (contact normal 20 to 45 degrees up). The bounce then took off horizontal speed, and the game charged it.
4. **Lamp posts and trees counted as walls.** These are "soft" props. Near the camera, the game hides them. One head-on post hit stopped the drone dead (the critic's "nothing in view looks like an obstacle", grass, 10 m from a park pad). In a probe I placed the drone in front of a park lamp post: it stopped at 0 m/s and stayed pinned.

## Design

- **Only real walls can cost you.** Only a wall contact counts toward a crash. These contacts are never counted:
  - a low lip the drone can skim (the same test the game uses for its hop)
  - an underside clip on an edge (normal y 0.35 to 0.7)
  - a lamp post or tree
- **Severity is the speed the collision itself takes off.** It is the horizontal speed before the sub-stepped collision loop minus the speed after it. Thrust, drag, braking and the guide all run before the loop, so none of them count. The 3, 4 and 5 s tiers and the 7 m/s threshold are unchanged.
- **A hop always wins.** The crash is judged 0.2 s after first contact (the same span the loss was already summed over). If the game's own ledge hop fires inside that span, the collision is a skim and costs nothing.
- **The pad's own edge is free.** The rule applies within 30 m of the current pad. A wall there is free if its top is within 1.5 m of the pad height and the skids are less than 4 m below that top. This covers a roof lip, a kerb or a lawn edge you clip as you drop in.
- **Low lines keep their speed.** This is the low-flying reward. I kept it inside the physics and added no new scoring rule.
  - Lips up to 0.9 m above the skids are stepped over at full speed. This covers parapets (0.7 m), kerbs and fountain rims. The body eases up visually.
  - Taller ledges up to 2.2 m still hop. The pop is faster the harder you meet the ledge. Once you clear the top, the hop gives back 85% of the forward speed it absorbed.
  - Posts and trees brush you aside with 80% of your speed. There is one glance per brush, so you slide off instead of sticking.
- **Round 4 feedback is unchanged for real crashes:** HUD pop, -Ns chip, vignette, sparks, shake, tumble and sound. Brushes and skims keep the light scrape feedback.

I did not add a slipstream or combo bonus. Fair skims plus speed carry already make the low line pay. A scoring rule needs its own balancing round.

# Drone Dash, round 4 builder report: crash stakes

## Design

A crash is measured by the speed it takes off the drone (horizontal m/s), summed over the first 0.2 s of one collision. That is the same thing the player feels and the same thing the critic measures.

| Speed lost in one hit | Name | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| under 7 m/s | scrape | free (old spark + thud only) |
| 7 to 16 m/s | Crash | -3 s, combo broken |
| 16 to 26 m/s | Hard crash | -4 s, combo broken |
| 26 m/s and up | Wreck | -5 s, combo broken |

Why this set:
- **Time plus combo, nothing else.** Time is the run's only life bar, so it always matters, even with no combo. The combo break is the sharp extra cost for players who are on a streak. Both are already on screen, so no new meter is needed. I left out parcel damage to keep it to one rule the player can learn.
- **Fair.** One charge per collision: follow-up contacts within 1.2 s only top up to a harder tier, so being pinned against a wall does not drain the clock. A slow slide along a parapet never adds up to a crash (only the first 0.2 s counts). A crash never takes the clock below 1 s, so a crash alone never ends the run.
- **Arrival work stays free.** The guide pull, ledge hop and braking near a pad move at low speed and lose well under 7 m/s. A ledge hop now reports its impact. A full-speed boost into a parapet stops the drone dead, so it is a crash. The gentle hop over a parapet is still free.
- **Readable in the moment.**
  - HUD: a "Hard crash!" pop in coral with "-4 SEC · X2.0 COMBO LOST" under it. A large coral "-4s" drops out beside the timer, and the timer shudders with a coral flash. The combo chip turns coral, keeps the lost multiplier, is struck through, then tips and falls away.

## Causes found

# Round 2 builder note: blank desktop render with bloom on

## Root cause
The objective sky beam (and its ring wall) shader computed `pow(1.0 - vUv.y, ...)`. The composer renders into a 4x MSAA HalfFloat target, and on partly covered edge pixels MSAA evaluates varyings at the pixel centre, so `vUv.y` lands just above 1.0 on the beam's top rim and `pow()` of a negative base returns NaN (about 15 to 18 pixels near the top of the screen). The clamp pass lets NaN through (NaN fails every comparison), and UnrealBloomPass blurs it across every mip, so the composite becomes NaN everywhere and the output shows one flat colour.

How it was proven: per-pass readback on a blank frame showed NaN first appearing in the RenderPass output (18 values), the high-pass target at 9 values, and the bloom output at 100% NaN. Hiding each visible object in turn on that frame showed that only hiding the beam mesh brought the NaN count to 0. The same frame rendered to non-MSAA Half or Float targets had no NaN, which matches the MSAA extrapolation explanation.

## Fix
`src/entities/ObjectiveMarker.ts`: clamp `vUv.y` (wall and beam) and `vFacing` (beam) to [0,1] before `pow()`. No change to the post chain, bloom settings or quality. The look is unchanged.

# Drone Dash, round 1 critic report

**Verdict:** A charming, well-built arcade courier game that is unplayable on desktop as shipped. With bloom on (the desktop default), the 3D view turns into a flat orange screen for most of the flight.

## The single biggest gap: the desktop render goes blank in flight

With the default desktop settings (post-processing on), the whole 3D view regularly turns into one flat peach-orange colour. Only the HUD stays visible. This happens while you climb, boost, hover high, and even hover still at 14 m. It lasts for seconds at a time, and some single frames flicker back to normal, so it strobes. With `?post=0` the same flight renders perfectly. On touch devices post-processing is off by default, so mobile does not have the bug.

Evidence:
- I read back pixels inside the page after each frame (9 sample points per frame, Apple M5 Metal GPU through ANGLE). While I held W+Space for 3 s, **187 of 331 frames (56%)** were one flat colour with post on. With `?post=1` it was 202/348, and with `?post=0` it was **0/361**. Script: `critic-scripts/pixel-probe.mjs`, `pixel-probe2.mjs`.
- Key probe (`critic-scripts/key-probe.mjs`): standing still and W alone render fine. After about 0.6 s of W+Space the view goes flat. W+E (boost) was flat for 6 of 8 screenshots.
- In a full autopilot run, my screenshot helper retried 25 times over about 1 s and still could not get a non-blank frame at four key moments: `critic/d03-flying-BLANK.png`, `d04-near-pickup-BLANK.png`, `d05-parcel-picked-BLANK.png`, `d08b-after-bump-BLANK.png`. Also `d10-blank-frame-post-on.png` and `probe-boost-held.png`.
- The same moments with post off look great: `critic/nopost-d03-flying.png`, `nopost-d08b-after-bump.png`, `nopost-d06-carrying-enroute.png`.
- The camera is not inside a building. During blank frames it was in open air at y 40 to 48 (logged in the probe run). The background colour you see is the sky/fog horizon colour, which means the post chain outputs a flat result. ## Other gaps for later rounds (ranked)
1. **Arrival is fiddly.** Pickup radius is 3.2 m and the vertical tolerance is tight. My autopilot circled a rooftop pickup 4.4 m away for over 4 s, and twice hovered on a roof edge 21 to 24 m from a street-level drop it could not drop down to (`nopost-d08-stuck-near-drop.png`). A good arcade courier game pulls you into the zone at the end of the approach, or shows a clear landing cone so the player knows where to descend.
2. **Collisions feel like walls of rubber, not crashes.** I logged 5 to 7 bumps per run. Speed drops from about 30 to 10 m/s with a small flash. There is no visible drone wobble or spin and no lasting penalty, so walls never feel dangerous. The timer is the only stake.
3. **Boost is tiny on mobile.** One 2 s boost press drained the bar almost to empty and flung the drone out over the water (`m03-boost-turn.png`). The boost button sits right next to climb, so it is easy to hit by mistake.
4. **Run start is disorienting.** At 0.3 s the title text is still fading over the gameplay view, and the target is 78 m away behind your left shoulder (`d02-takeoff.png`). The drone should spawn facing the first pickup with the camera already behind it.
5. **No sense of difficulty curve.** Three legs per run, all of the same kind. Time bonuses (+19 s) make the clock generous early and nothing ramps up. Two deliveries scored 1,188 and earned "New best" (`d09-game-over.png`).

What already meets the bar: the golden-hour art direction, readable building silhouettes, the drone model with banking, the strong objective beam and pad ring (`nopost-d08b-after-bump.png`), the edge-of-screen nav arrow with distance, the clear pop text and combo chip (`d07-delivery.png`), and a clean game-over card. The best polished browser arcade flyers have a camera that never hides the target, a world that reads at a glance, landings that snap in, and impacts you feel in the controller. The main things missing here are the stable render and the landing snap.
