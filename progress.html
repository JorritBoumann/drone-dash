# Drone Dash, round 5 builder report: fair crash classification

## Cause

The round 4 crash check fired on the game's own skim helpers. Four separate faults caused this:

1. **The ledge hop counted as a crash.** A hop cancels the drone's speed into the ledge. The crash check then counted that speed as "lost".
2. **The speed loss was a raw before/after delta.** It included drag, the S brake and the drop guide's pull near pads. So near a pad, a light touch plus braking was enough to pass 7 m/s.
3. **Edge clips counted as wall hits.** Sometimes the drone's underside clipped a roof edge or the rim of a box (contact normal 20 to 45 degrees up). The bounce then took off horizontal speed, and the game charged it.
4. **Lamp posts and trees counted as walls.** These are "soft" props. Near the camera, the game hides them. One head-on post hit stopped the drone dead (the critic's "nothing in view looks like an obstacle", grass, 10 m from a park pad). In a probe I placed the drone in front of a park lamp post: it stopped at 0 m/s and stayed pinned.

## Design

- **Only real walls can cost you.** Only a wall contact counts toward a crash. These contacts are never counted:
  - a low lip the drone can skim (the same test the game uses for its hop)
  - an underside clip on an edge (normal y 0.35 to 0.7)
  - a lamp post or tree
- **Severity is the speed the collision itself takes off.** It is the horizontal speed before the sub-stepped collision loop minus the speed after it. Thrust, drag, braking and the guide all run before the loop, so none of them count. The 3, 4 and 5 s tiers and the 7 m/s threshold are unchanged.
- **A hop always wins.** The crash is judged 0.2 s after first contact (the same span the loss was already summed over). If the game's own ledge hop fires inside that span, the collision is a skim and costs nothing.
- **The pad's own edge is free.** The rule applies within 30 m of the current pad. A wall there is free if its top is within 1.5 m of the pad height and the skids are less than 4 m below that top. This covers a roof lip, a kerb or a lawn edge you clip as you drop in.
- **Low lines keep their speed.** This is the low-flying reward. I kept it inside the physics and added no new scoring rule.
  - Lips up to 0.9 m above the skids are stepped over at full speed. This covers parapets (0.7 m), kerbs and fountain rims. The body eases up visually.
  - Taller ledges up to 2.2 m still hop. The pop is faster the harder you meet the ledge. Once you clear the top, the hop gives back 85% of the forward speed it absorbed.
  - Posts and trees brush you aside with 80% of your speed. There is one glance per brush, so you slide off instead of sticking.
- **Round 4 feedback is unchanged for real crashes:** HUD pop, -Ns chip, vignette, sparks, shake, tumble and sound. Brushes and skims keep the light scrape feedback.

I did not add a slipstream or combo bonus. Fair skims plus speed carry already make the low line pay. A scoring rule needs its own balancing round.

# Drone Dash, round 4 builder report: crash stakes

## Design

A crash is measured by the speed it takes off the drone (horizontal m/s), summed over the first 0.2 s of one collision. That is the same thing the player feels and the same thing the critic measures.

| Speed lost in one hit | Name | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| under 7 m/s | scrape | free (old spark + thud only) |
| 7 to 16 m/s | Crash | -3 s, combo broken |
| 16 to 26 m/s | Hard crash | -4 s, combo broken |
| 26 m/s and up | Wreck | -5 s, combo broken |

Why this set:
- **Time plus combo, nothing else.** Time is the run's only life bar, so it always matters, even with no combo. The combo break is the sharp extra cost for players who are on a streak. Both are already on screen, so no new meter is needed. I left out parcel damage to keep it to one rule the player can learn.
- **Fair.** One charge per collision: follow-up contacts within 1.2 s only top up to a harder tier, so being pinned against a wall does not drain the clock. A slow slide along a parapet never adds up to a crash (only the first 0.2 s counts). A crash never takes the clock below 1 s, so a crash alone never ends the run.
- **Arrival work stays free.** The guide pull, ledge hop and braking near a pad move at low speed and lose well under 7 m/s. A ledge hop now reports its impact. A full-speed boost into a parapet stops the drone dead, so it is a crash. The gentle hop over a parapet is still free.
- **Readable in the moment.**
  - HUD: a "Hard crash!" pop in coral with "-4 SEC · X2.0 COMBO LOST" under it. A large coral "-4s" drops out beside the timer, and the timer shudders with a coral flash. The combo chip turns coral, keeps the lost multiplier, is struck through, then tips and falls away.

# Round 2 builder note: blank desktop render with bloom on

## Root cause
The objective sky beam (and its ring wall) shader computed `pow(1.0 - vUv.y, ...)`. The composer renders into a 4x MSAA HalfFloat target, and on partly covered edge pixels MSAA evaluates varyings at the pixel centre, so `vUv.y` lands just above 1.0 on the beam's top rim and `pow()` of a negative base returns NaN (about 15 to 18 pixels near the top of the screen). The clamp pass lets NaN through (NaN fails every comparison), and UnrealBloomPass blurs it across every mip, so the composite becomes NaN everywhere and the output shows one flat colour.

How it was proven: per-pass readback on a blank frame showed NaN first appearing in the RenderPass output (18 values), the high-pass target at 9 values, and the bloom output at 100% NaN. Hiding each visible object in turn on that frame showed that only hiding the beam mesh brought the NaN count to 0. The same frame rendered to non-MSAA Half or Float targets had no NaN, which matches the MSAA extrapolation explanation.

## Fix
`src/entities/ObjectiveMarker.ts`: clamp `vUv.y` (wall and beam) and `vFacing` (beam) to [0,1] before `pow()`. No change to the post chain, bloom settings or quality. The look is unchanged.

# Drone Dash, round 1 critic report

**Verdict:** A charming, well-built arcade courier game that is unplayable on desktop as shipped. With bloom on (the desktop default), the 3D view turns into a flat orange screen for most of the flight.

## The single biggest gap: the desktop render goes blank in flight

With the default desktop settings (post-processing on), the whole 3D view regularly turns into one flat peach-orange colour. Only the HUD stays visible. This happens while you climb, boost, hover high, and even hover still at 14 m. It lasts for seconds at a time, and some single frames flicker back to normal, so it strobes.
